Rabbit Royale: The Cursed Crown — GDD v0.9
Competitive persistent minesweeper. Dig carrots, stack them in your burrow, raid other burrows. The #1 wears a crown that makes them everyone's target.
Minesweeper × Clash of Clans, on Solana Mobile. Free-to-play, zero wagering, no cash out. Android + Solana Seeker dApp store. Reuses the live Rabbit Royale codebase, pixel art and island engine.

Core loop
DIG (5-10 min runs) → STACK (burrow, async) → RAID (PvP) → repeat. While you play you can only win. The only thing you can lose sits in your burrow, while you're away.
The Run
Drop-in islands, up to 4 rabbits, no lobby ever. A joiner lands on the FULLEST island that still has room — players cluster, because drop-in without a lobby only feels alive when the empty island is not the default. Full, or too far gone to be worth a run → a new one spawns.
Shared board: a revealed cell is revealed for all, first to dig gets the carrot. Rabbits move cell by cell, visibly — the race is physical.
Energy is the FUEL OF EXPLORING, on a bar of 300 (100 from 17 September 2026, 150 then 300 on the 21st) — and it always runs out for a player who does not read the board. Walking dug ground is free; digging a fresh tile costs 1; a bomb costs 30. Carrots, golden ones included, are score and never fuel: only a right red X puts energy back.
The red X is what stretches a run. A player may mark any undug, unread tile around their rabbit as a bomb, and the server answers at once. Right: energy back — +3 on Meadow and Thicket, +2 on Ashland and Caldera, less as the bombs thicken because bombs are a reader's fuel and one price for every tier starves the first island and floods the last — plus a small carrot bounty that climbs with the streak; the bomb stays buried under its X and nobody can step on it. Wrong: −15, the streak is gone, and the tile's number is written on it. Energy is capped at 100; what a full bar cannot take is paid as carrots, so a right X always pays something.
THE CAPPED RUN. A perfect marker earns back 50-63 % of what the digging cost, on every tier, so reading the board roughly DOUBLES a run and never makes it endless: on the 300 bar about 250 tiles without the X, 420 with it — twelve minutes to twenty-five. A run that reads nothing ends on the same sentence at every level — no energy, no more exploring — and on the recap that offers more. Skill pays in carrots per ticket, not in time without end: on the same bar a reader brings home twice the walker's carrots (999 to 399 on Meadow, 1 365 to 352 on Caldera). THE GAME REWARDS THE PLAYER WHO THINKS (Paul, 21 September 2026), and on the first week's islands thinking means FINISHING the island: a reader clears Meadow and Thicket alone in one run, ending on the last chest with fuel that comes home and pays the next crossing. From Ashland on, even a reader dies — that is where the difficulty starts, and where the recap sells the refill to everyone; the walker sees it on every tier. Measured before deciding (tools/sim-dig.sim.ts): a bigger island does nothing (the disc already fills the grid), a heavier bomb punishes the walker and leaves the reader at 99 %, and only an X at +1 on the first tiers makes the tank bind again, at the price of telling a new player that reading pays almost nothing on the island where the X is taught — rejected. To confirm in playtests with real readers: does finishing Meadow in one go bore, and how much fuel comes home. An island is a level several runs finish, alone over its day or four rabbits at once, from Ashland on; on Meadow and Thicket, one reader can. With a generous X (+8) a good reader never ran dry, cleared the island in fifteen minutes on one 20-energy ticket and never once saw the refill offer.
Energy is elastic, never random: every amount is fixed and displayed. The loss is five times the gain or more, so a blind X only pays above five chances in six — guessing loses, knowing wins — and it is half a bomb, so at a true coin-flip an X is the cheaper way to find out.
Why a bar again, after three days of hearts. Hearts (24 points, a bomb took 8, digging free) stated the rule in one look, and made the numbers optional: simulated with robot players on real islands (tools/sim-dig.sim.ts), a player who deduced NOTHING still cleared 83 % of a Meadow island. A heart cannot show a third of itself, so the bar came back — yellow, with the battery bolt, standing on its own above the board.
Energy per dig WAS rejected once, and the reason still holds for what was rejected: a cost with no way to earn it back, on a bar of 30, made a run seventeen tiles long and ended it by attrition. The cost that shipped is a different machine — a hundred points, and a pump the player operates by solving the puzzle. Dig 2 was tried in simulation and rejected again: Meadow has too few bombs to pay for it, which made the first tier the hardest.
The cascade reaches three squares from the rabbit and is carried on by walking, so an island is no longer born a quarter read. Bombs and golden carrots thicken with the walk from the spawn (same counts, weighted outward), and a bomb is dealt touching at most one other, which spreads the same bombs over more of the board.
Zero ends the run, whatever emptied the bar — a blast, a wrong X or the digging itself. No energy, no more exploring: one rule, and the recap that follows is where a refill is offered. The X can save a run, but it has to do it before the bar is empty: when the bar drops to one bomb's worth a line says so and the X button beats from there on, so the end is a countdown the player can answer rather than a surprise.
Bomb: knockback + stun + 30 energy. Nothing is stolen mid-run, no hard death.
Loot is random (carrot value, chests: items, NFTs); danger is deterministic (clue numbers). Risk is skill, reward is lottery.
Chests hold ONE table wherever they are dug: carrots (the floor, ~40% — every other line can be capped out or unwanted, and a chest that disappoints is worse than no chest because the player walked onto a known tile for it), raid items (bomb, shield, lightning), the two garden consumables (water and fertiliser, see The Burrow), and an NFT at 1 in 100. One table rather than a raid table and a farming one: a farmer who never raids still pulls something they want three times in ten, and splitting it would sort players into two games that never trade.
Four islands, unlocked by lifetime carrots: Meadow, Thicket, Ashland, Caldera. Each is richer AND more dangerous than the last. Since the red X, bombs are also FUEL for a player who can prove them, so what hardens up the ladder is not the count of bombs but the corners no number resolves: a reader is forced to guess 6 times on a Meadow island and 63 on Caldera (simulated). The doors are at 6 000, 19 500 and 37 500 lifetime carrots: days 3, 8 and 14 for a regular player, computed by tools/economy-day.sim.ts from MEASURED run income rather than from a model of a rabbit digging blind (21 tiles, 120 carrots), which is what the first thresholds came from.
The whole ladder is walked in about a fortnight of ordinary play, half a season. Seeing every island the game has is not the long game — the burrow ladder is. The crown should be decided between players who all know the hardest board, not between players still unlocking it.
Lifetime never resets, so the tiers are an account's progression rather than a season's: a returning player starts season two already in Caldera. Revisit if picking your island per run ever ships (see Cursed Islands).
Island life cycle: no re-covering. THE ISLAND IS A LEVEL — it is cleared when every CHEST has been taken. The chests sit around the island's rim, so clearing one means walking its whole edge. Then the sea comes up, the island goes under, and every run on it ENDS: carrots banked, "Island cleared!", back to the burrow. Nobody is ever asked to step on a bomb to finish: the chests are all on open, readable ground.

WHY THE SEA AND NOT A VOLCANO. An island that sinks because it has been emptied is the same sentence as the game's: rabbits dig an island until there is nothing left holding it up. A volcano was a second, unrelated threat bolted to the side of that — a mountain nobody dug, deciding on its own to end a run about carrots. The tide is the island's own accounting, and it is already on screen: the sea is drawn around every board.
The sinking used to move the survivors onto a fresh island, energy and carrots intact. With free digging that made a run endless for anyone who avoided the bombs — no clock, no board limit, nothing to end it. The island IS the clock now.
A started island is not thrown away. It lives on for a day with nobody on it, so the player who died on it — or anyone else — can come back, pay a run, and finish what is left. What remains to dig is exactly what that run is worth, and that is a real choice: a fresh island is a long run, a half-dug one is a short cheap haul.
Below ~20 safe cells left, no new player is sent to it (a run of a dozen cells is not worth what a run costs) and, once empty, it is torn down at once. Whoever is still on it digs to the end.
Cursed Islands (opt-in mode): no clue numbers, higher variance rewards. Same average yield as normal islands — a thrill choice, never the optimal farm.
The Burrow
One building, one level. Leveling up = garden produces more + bigger defense budget.
ONE TANK (21 September 2026). The bar the medallion shows IS the run's fuel IS the raid's: 300 points, spent by the dig, the bomb, the wrong X, the raid's toll, step and trap, refilled by the right X, by time (30 an hour, empty to full in 10 hours: sized to the gap between sessions, so two sit-downs a day each find a full tank and a third finds a raid and half a run) and by the shop. Crossing to an island takes a small fee (5) behind a floor (40: a blast survived with something left to read, 80 minutes of regen after a dead run); the rabbit leaves with what the tank holds and BRINGS HOME what is left. Since a run ends at zero, a full tank is one run: the day is 720 points of energy, and the refill sold in the shop is one more run — priced at 700 carrots, just under what a run pays, so it stays a sink without being a joke. Regen, refill and the tier doors all FOLLOW the bar (tools/economy-day.sim.ts prints where they land). Candy Crush's five lives, with a rabbit on top.
LOOKING IS NOT PLAYING. The crossing is charged at the door, because that is the only moment the bank is in hand — but what it buys is a board, and a board nobody stepped on was never dealt. A player who crosses, takes not one step and walks home gets the run's cost back on the way out; one step, dug or merely walked, and it is paid. The test is having MOVED, not having dug (walking open ground is free, and a rabbit spawns beside ground that is already open, so a real run can dig nothing) and not the haul (an unlucky board is a run that happened). The refund folds in what regenerated meanwhile and stops at the ceiling, so crossing and waiting is worth exactly nothing. A rabbit shoved onto a bomb before it ever moved is refunded too: its run was ended by somebody else.
A new player is created with a FULL bank. Nobody discovers this game half-empty: the first session is the one that decides whether there is a second.
Garden: passive carrot production, capped (~15% of active income — the run stays the main source). Harvest on return; stealable if you don't.
Water and fertiliser (chest drops, never sold): the garden's two dials, and they are deliberately different ones. WATER raises the RATE for a few hours — it pays the player who comes back often and harvests before the cap. FERTILISER raises the CEILING for longer — it pays the player who cannot come back tonight. One rewards attention, the other forgives its absence, so a player holding both has a real decision about the day ahead rather than a strictly-better button.
Both are windows of TIME, not instant top-ups: an instant "+N carrots" would be a carrot drop wearing a different sprite, whereas a window makes using one a small bet on when you will next be here. A second drop extends a running window rather than restarting it, and banked time is capped — an uncapped stack would be a permanently buffed garden, which is just a higher base rate with extra steps.
Neither is ever for sale. They are the reason a farmer opens a chest, and the moment water is on the shelf a rich player buys a permanent garden and the drop stops meaning anything. This is the one place the Economy's "buyable in carrots OR money" rule does not reach — because it is a reward for digging, not a good.
Stock: exposed above a FLOOR. A raid takes a slice of what stands above it and can never reach below — the floor is what a player feels when they read "safe stock", and it is the beginner's protection (Clash of Clans does the same job with loot caps per town hall). It is not secured storage: it does not scale, so a king cannot bunker his score behind it. New players also get a starting shield (~3 days).
The GARDEN is the other purse, and the one a raid is really for. What is left growing outside is raided at a much higher share than what is banked inside — Clash of Clans pillages collectors at 50% where storages give 10-20%, for exactly this reason: what is produced passively and left out should be what is vulnerable. Before this, a raid took a slice of the stock and never touched the garden, so letting carrots grow was free and being away was never punished. Now a full garden is worth walking in for, which is what brings its owner home twice a day to bring it in.
Stolen garden carrots cost the victim no season score — they had not been harvested, so they were never scored. The thief still receives the lot.
Defense field: your burrow is a 15x15 board with a fixed shape — the door, the stone path and the carrot field are where the art puts them. You bury traps on the walkable ground between them (budget per level, a valid path must always remain). Every burrow has the same shape; what differs between two of them is where the owner buried, which is the whole point. Your base IS a puzzle you design.
Traps are invisible to a raider until sprung. A visible trap is just a wall, and a wall gets routed around rather than feared.
The DOORSTEP — the first steps inside the entrance (TRAPS.DOORSTEP of them, measured as the raid measures, in steps) — cannot be mined, and BOTH sides are shown where the door is and how far the doorstep runs. With the door minable, every burrow had the same best defence, a ring of bombs round the tile the raider lands on, and the crossing ended before a clue was read. The defender now builds behind the doorstep, where the raider has to walk into it; the raider sees where they start and where the reading begins. The ring round the field is never doorstep, whatever the constant says.
Smoke screen (defensive, expensive): hides the clue numbers of YOUR burrow for 24h. A raider crosses it blind, reading nothing but their own steps. It is the anti-revenge item — after you take someone's carrots, it is what stops them walking straight back in with your layout memorised. Priced high on purpose: the crossing is meant to be solvable, and permanent blindness would make defence free.
Repairs and defense re-setup are free. Always.
A sprung trap REARMS ON A CLOCK; it is never re-bought and never re-placed. The tile keeps its trap, only the arming is on a timer, and the burrow comes back up one trap at a time over a few hours. This is the concrete form of "re-setup is free": a trap the owner already earned stays theirs, so the free daily allowance is what EXTENDS a defence rather than what repairs last night's.
The alternative — a sprung trap consumed outright — is pay-to-repair wearing a different name, and it is rejected for the reason listed below. The asymmetry is what makes it fatal: loss has no rate limit (as many raids as there are attackers) while replacement is capped at the daily allowance, so a player raided overnight cannot re-arm as fast as they are emptied and each morning starts poorer than the last. Clash of Clans re-arms free and automatically on login for exactly this reason.
Rearming is GRADUAL rather than instant, because a burrow that snaps back to full the moment its owner logs in makes the second raider's night meaningless. Partial recovery also reads better: coming back to three traps of eight standing is progress in motion, where an empty floor is a chore at zero.
LOSING carrots raises a SHIELD, automatically — being visited does not. A raid that ended with nothing in the sack (an empty burrow, a garden already brought in) did its owner no harm, and a shield for it was a reward for having nothing worth stealing; until loot actually leaves, a burrow stays open to be raided. Rearming alone does not close the farm window — it only refills the burrow behind an attacker who has already left. The shield is what stops the queue forming while the owner is asleep, and it is the same absolute protection a new player's starting shield gives. Depth still grades it: a sacked field buys longer than a raid stopped short. Note the loot floor (a raid that dies on the doorstep still takes a share), so in practice a stocked burrow is shielded by almost any finished raid; only an empty one is not.
The fog never carries between raiders. Each attacker crosses the burrow blind, reading only their own steps — what raider n+1 inherits is the STATE of the floor (traps still sprung), never the KNOWLEDGE of it. Handing over the previous crossing would stack an intel advantage on top of a defence that is already down, and the defender loses twice for one absence.
PvP
The Crown: leaderboard #1 is marked on the world map, gets a score bonus, and drops more loot. Being first = being hunted.
Sabotage (real-time): spectate a live run, plant a signed bomb or lightning on their island. Items are earned in chests or bought, never free. The victim sees who did it — revenge is the point. A planted bomb updates revealed numbers: sharp players can spot the "2" that became a "3".
Raid (async): you enter the defender's base and WALK THEIR BOARD, tile by tile, from the door towards the carrot field — the minefield they designed. You spend energy on every step; a sprung trap drains a chunk of it. At zero the raid ends where you stand.
The raid keeps a BUDGET, not hearts, and deliberately so: a step has to cost something, or the distance the defender put between the door and the field defends nothing and only the traps count. On the island the cost is the bombs; in a burrow it is the walk.
A RAID BEGINS AT THE FIRST STEP, not when the target is opened. Opening one is how you look at it — the entrance and its neighbours are all a raider sees until they move — so a raid abandoned without a step is not a raid: it costs the burrow no energy, starts no cooldown on the victim, counts for no quest and no tally, writes nothing in the victim's log, and the defender is never told anyone came. The moment a step lands, all of it counts, whatever happens next — and the ONE TANK pays a toll (45) then every step (1) and every sprung trap (8), capped at a STAKE of 75 — the toll plus a 30-point walk — so a full tank cannot walk through five traps without feeling them; a tank short of the toll plus the longest crossing (13 steps) is refused at the door, beside the wait and the refill, never on the board. The toll is sized so a raid is still the better trade per point of energy — about twice a run, not the three to five times it was at 15 — because the two doors now draw on the same points; it follows the bar, since the haul grows with everyone's income. This is the burrow's "looking is not playing" rule seen from the other door.
A raid is scored by HOW FAR you got, not by whether you "won". The crossing is only a handful of steps, so a pass/fail rule would resolve every raid to 100% or 0% however it was tuned; paying by depth makes each trap shave a slice off the haul instead of deciding the whole thing. Reach the field → the full share of both purses. Die on the doorstep → a floor, so attacking a defended burrow is never pure loss and nobody stops attacking after one bad run. A single raid is capped whatever it found, and the garden fills that cap first.
Attackers can invite a friend to dig together; the defender gets a push notification and can rush home.
Economy
A dug carrot is worth MANY carrots, not one. At one apiece a whole run paid two carrots against a garden growing hundreds a day: playing was ~1% of the income, so the optimal strategy was to not play and wait for the garden. The three ways to earn — run, garden, raid — are tuned to carry comparable weight over a day, so none of them is the one right answer.
One carrot feeds three counters:
Wallet — spendable, stealable. Buys everything in-game.
Season score — the leaderboard. Spending never touches it; only theft moves it: stolen carrots leave your score and join the thief's. The King can be dethroned by raids. Spending surplus = protecting points.
Lifetime — never resets. Doubles as XP: levels, richer islands, lore chapters.
Everything on the shelf is buyable in carrots OR money (water and fertiliser are not on the shelf — see The Burrow). Crypto (SOL/USDC via Seeker) is the convenience route; carrot prices are steep on energy (deliberate sink). No exclusive power for money, ever. Cosmetics and NFT drops bridge visually with the existing PFP collection — no economic bridge.
Seasons & narrative — The Cursed Crown
Seasons last 1 month. Season score resets; lifetime and stock never do.
The island is alive: it gives carrots and demands a King. At season's end, the #1 faces the Sacrifice, live in front of the server:
Accept → tomb of honor, unique NFT, writes his own epitaph.
Flee → 50/50 on-chain coin flip (provably fair, no wager). Win: return as the Errant King — crowned, hunted, shieldless. Lose: a runaway's tomb. Each escape lowers the next odds: the island learns.
Mausoleum of Kings: every fallen King gets a permanent tomb in the world with his name and last words. Resets erase scores, never glory.
Lore drips with lifetime milestones and chest fragments (Hades model: even bad sessions advance the story).
Design pillars (and what we rejected)
Deterministic risk, random reward. Session length is the skill reward.
Being rich = being visible = being hunted, at every scale (crown, loot, sabotage targets).
Rules and lore tell the same story (the crown is literally cursed).
Rejected: mid-run loot loss & cashout (gambling leftovers) · spending lowering rank (kills the economy) · secured storage (unkillable kings) · pay-to-repair (churn) · board re-covering (breaks minesweeper logic) · random energy amounts (slot machine) · energy per dig WITH NO WAY TO EARN IT BACK (an egg timer that hides the bombs behind attrition — the red X is what makes the shipped dig cost a different thing) · a higher energy ceiling (a reader just sits at the new ceiling, and the last tension goes) · an X generous enough to sustain a run (+8: the run never ends for a reader, the refill offer is never seen, runs swell to 85 % of all income) · golden carrots that give energy back (39 of them on a Caldera island out-fuel any X) · a "dry" state at zero energy where only a right X revives the rabbit (shipped for an hour on 17 September 2026: three sentences to explain, proven bombs hoarded as a reserve tank, and the refill offer gone from the moment it matters) · surrounding a bomb to defuse it (paid twice for the same knowledge the X pays for) · a red ring on unread tiles (unexplained colour; the "?" says it instead) · a run that survives its island (nothing is left to end it once digging is free) · a timer on a run (the island is the clock, and a clock would cut a player off mid-board) · charging a crossing that was never walked (a third of the bank for a look, seen live on 21 September 2026) · a shield for a raid that took nothing (a reward for having nothing worth stealing) · telling a defender about a raider who never stepped in (a siren for a visit that never happened).
To tune in prototype
Dig cost, bomb cost, X gain and loss, and what a golden carrot gives back (measure with tools/sim-dig.sim.ts before moving one) · bomb density (it is a reader's fuel as well as the danger) · the cascade's reach · the carrot bounty on an X and the overflow rate · burrow energy: bank size, refill rate, cost of a crossing · island size vs. run length (a lone player clears a whole island; four share it) · the join floor in cells · how long a started island waits for someone to finish it · what a dug carrot is worth, and the island tiers that were spaced when it was worth one · the safe floor & the garden's raidable share · defense field size & bomb budgets per level · the doorstep (steps inside the door no bomb may sit under; it moves the generator, so it lands with a deploy) · Cursed Island variance · garden yield curve · chest density & loot weights · water/fertiliser strength and duration · trap rearm delay & whether it staggers per trap · post-raid shield duration.

A dug carrot is worth 3 (it was 15): that single value is what re-seated the economy on 17 September 2026, because everything else priced in carrots had been tuned for a run worth about what a raid or a garden visit is, and a played run digs 100-200 tiles where the model dug 21. At 3: a run ≈ 170 without the X, ≈ 500 for a reader, beside a 240 garden visit and a ≈ 200 raid; runs are 65 % of a regular day (ceiling 70), raids 10 % (floor 10).
